## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment (Assessment A):** Authored for the brand, not a reskinned template — the "Nova" (stellar explosion) metaphor is carried literally through the starfield canvas, cosmic gradients, a comet-icon floating CTA, and "вибухового старту" copy, a coherent chain a generic swap wouldn't produce. Portfolio images are real AI-generated output, not stock photos, and per-service copy is genuinely differentiated. Where it drifts generic: the icon set (hexagon-node, play-rect, speech-bubble — standard line-icon-library fare) and the page skeleton (hero → about → services → portfolio → process → advantages → CTA), which is the shape any B2B agency site would use.

**Deterministic scan (Assessment B):** `detect.mjs` static scan of `index.html`: 0 findings, exit 0 — clean. The **live browser overlay** (`detect.js` injected into the running page, not the static HTML scanner) reported 54 anti-patterns: ~47 `low-contrast` findings and 3 `dark-glow` findings measured against a `#ffffff`/`#fff2ed` background, plus 5 `icon-tile-stack` findings (one per service card).

**False positives, verified:** The `low-contrast` findings are a detector artifact, not real failures. I independently confirmed `getComputedStyle(document.body).backgroundColor` returns `rgba(0,0,0,0)` (fully transparent) — the site's actual dark background comes entirely from a `background: radial-gradient(...)` shorthand and layered `body::before`/`::after` pseudo-elements, neither of which a naive ancestor-walking `backgroundColor` check can see. With no resolvable background anywhere in the chain, the checker fell back to assuming white, which is why every "low-contrast" hit reads near-white text against `#ffffff`/`#fff2ed`/`#d2af90` — colors that don't exist as literal painted backgrounds anywhere on this page. Earlier this session I computed real WCAG contrast ratios against the actual rendered palette (near-black `#050403` background): every text/background pair passes AA, several pass AAA (5.03–17.95:1). Do not act on the 47 `low-contrast` findings.

**Real, worth weighing:** The 3 `dark-glow` findings (zero-offset text-shadow/box-shadow glows in coral) and especially the 5 `icon-tile-stack` findings (a square icon tile stacked above an h3, repeated identically across all 6 service cards) are legitimate pattern matches — and the `icon-tile-stack` finding directly corroborates both the original human feedback ("картки, картки, текст — дуже шаблонно") and Assessment A's independent cognitive-load/persona findings about the 6 similar service cards. This is the one finding triangulated across all three inputs (human reviewer, LLM design review, deterministic detector) — the highest-confidence item in this whole critique.

## Overall Impression

The structural and emotional design work (peak-end arc, brand-metaphor consistency, accessibility plumbing) is genuinely stronger than the site's remaining surface issues suggest. The single biggest opportunity is fixing the inverted hero CTA — a real, verified bug, not a matter of taste — followed by giving the 6 service cards more differentiation than "icon tile + heading" repeated six times, which both a human reviewer and an automated detector flagged independently.

## What's Working

- **The emotional arc is real and deliberate**: About's deliberate emptiness, the Poster section's 128px climax, and the one paper-color break at Contact are load-bearing decisions, not incidental, and the CSS comments narrate the reasoning directly.
- **Accessibility plumbing is above the bar for a course project**: every interactive control has a language-aware `aria-label`, form errors carry `aria-live`/`aria-invalid`/`aria-describedby`, global focus-visible rings exist, and touch targets were specifically audited to 44px.
- **The brand metaphor is carried through, not skin-deep**: "Nova" (launch/explosion) shows up consistently across the starfield background, the headline, and the floating CTA's comet icon.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Hero CTA visual hierarchy is inverted on the main landing page — and inconsistent with the site's own pattern.** On `index.html` lines 63-64, `btn-primary` (the loud glowing pill) is bound to `hero.ctaSecondary` ("Дізнатись більше" — the low-commitment action), while `btn-secondary` (the quiet glass pill) is bound to `hero.ctaPrimary` ("Залишити заявку" — the actual conversion goal). Verified this is a real inconsistency: all 6 service-detail pages (`ai-visuals.html`, `ai-video.html`, `ai-social.html`, `ai-automation.html`, `ai-copywriting.html`, `ai-consulting.html`) correctly give `btn-primary` to `hero.ctaPrimary`. Only the main landing page — the page most visitors hit first — has it backwards. **Why it matters:** the visually loudest, first-in-DOM CTA is steering visitors toward "read more" instead of "get in touch," on a Persuade-mode page where conversion is the whole point. **Fix:** swap the two classes on `index.html` lines 63-64 to match the pattern already used correctly on the 6 detail pages. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify` or a direct fix.

**[P1] Invalid form fields have no visible field-level error state.** `js/form.js` correctly sets `aria-invalid="true"` on failed validation, but confirmed zero CSS rules anywhere reference `[aria-invalid]` — the only visible signal is 12px pink text below the field, not the input itself. **Why it matters:** a user scanning quickly after a failed submit can miss which field needs fixing. **Fix:** add a border/box-shadow rule on `.field input[aria-invalid="true"]`, `.field textarea[aria-invalid="true"]`. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`.

**[P1] The 6 service cards read as a template: icon tile + heading, repeated six times.** Corroborated three ways — the human reviewer's original complaint, Assessment A's cognitive-load checklist (6-way unranked choice with no default), and Assessment B's detector (`icon-tile-stack` fired once per card, 5 hits). This is the strongest-evidence finding in the whole critique. Despite the varied card sizing/photos work done this session, the icon+heading pairing at the top of every card is still visually identical across all 6. **Fix:** vary the icon treatment itself (not just card size) — different icon scale, position, or a distinguishing visual per card, not just a uniform tile. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill` or `/impeccable layout`.

**[P2] Unranked 6-way choice at both the service picker and the interest checkboxes.** Six near-identical bento cards and six equal-weight form checkboxes give a first-time visitor no default or "not sure" path. **Fix:** add a low-commitment fallback option, or a stated ranking criterion for the bento sizing. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`.

**[P3] Logo is not a home/top link.** `<span class="logo">` on both `index.html` occurrences (headers, lines 39/248) breaks the near-universal "click wordmark to go home" convention. **Fix:** wrap in `<a href="#hero">`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (confused first-timer):** Lands on the hero; the loudest button says "Дізнатись більше" (read more), not "Залишити заявку" (contact us) — steered away from the actual goal. After following it to Services, hits six visually similar cards with no "start here" signal for someone who doesn't already know which of the six AI services they need.

**Riley (deliberate stress tester):** Submits the contact form empty — gets three small pink error strings, but input borders stay neutral (`var(--border-glass)`, confirmed no `[aria-invalid]` CSS exists), so a quick re-scan doesn't show which field is still wrong. Fills correctly and submits — `form.js` just sets `form.hidden = true` client-side (this is documented, intentional behavior: no backend exists), but there's no path back to the form without a full reload if Riley wants to send a second message.

**Casey (distracted mobile user):** At 375px the full path is hero → about → 6 cards → 6 portfolio tiles → 5 process steps → 5 advantage rows → poster → contact — a long scroll. Real mitigation exists: `.floating-cta` stays fixed at every scroll depth and jumps straight to `#contact`. Weak point: it's icon-only (comet SVG), label only in `aria-label` — a distracted user has to infer "this sends a message" from the icon alone.
